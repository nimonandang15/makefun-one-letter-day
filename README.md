**C — for "chaos".**

## Why C

The judge reads two letters at a time and picks the one whose hidden word
feels more vivid, funnier, or more true. The letter has to carry the word on
its own, so the strongest pick is the letter whose most natural word is
already a complete picture of a day.

- **Vivid and specific.** "Chaos" calls up a sharp picture at once: a day
  where everything happens at the same time and nothing goes to plan.
  It beats vague mood letters (J for joyful, G for glowing, V for
  victorious) because it describes what the day *was like*, not just how
  it felt.
- **Funny and surprising.** Chaos is self-deprecating in exactly the way
  people retell a bad day to a friend — it lands immediately.
- **True.** Everyone has had a chaos day, so it reads as an honest report,
  not a performance.
- **The letter fits the word.** C is the most immediate initial for
  "chaos"; no competing C-word describes a day as directly. Contrast S,
  which splits between "survived", "stressed" and "sleepy", or E, whose
  "exhausted" is vivid and true but far less funny.
- **Not the crowded examples.** The description's own examples — A for
  amazing, F for Friday-feeling, Z for zero sleep — are the obvious picks
  and will be the most duplicated. C dodges them while keeping a word
  just as instantly recognisable.
